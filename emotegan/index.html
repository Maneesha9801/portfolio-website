The Problem

Voice assistants (Siri/Alexa) are audibly intelligent but visually "blind." When we try to give them faces, they fall into the Uncanny Valley—they look robotic, display "dead eyes," and lack genuine emotional reactivity.

The Design Challenge

How can we engineer a generative system that doesn't just "lip-sync" audio, but actually performs emotion to create a genuine human connection?

The Engineering

I treated algorithmic failures as User Experience bugs. I identified three critical "Trust Breakers" and engineered specific technical solutions.

Intervention A: Fixing the "Dead Eye" Stare

The UX Friction: Standard GANs ignore eye movement, creating a "zombie-like" stare.

The Solution: Integrated MTCNN to isolate the eye region and devised a specific Eye Loss Function to penalize gaze drift.

Intervention B: Solving "Blurry Trust"

The UX Friction: Low-res 128px output signals "low quality."

The Solution: Engineered a post-processing pipeline using BSRGAN to upscale to crisp 512x512 video.

Ethics & Bias

Responsible AI Design

Generative AI carries inherent risks of deepfakes and bias. We implemented strict safeguards:

  • 1.
    Consent-Driven DataAll training faces were sourced from consented actor datasets (CREMA-D), not scraped from the web.
  • 2.
    WatermarkingAll generated videos are invisibly watermarked to prevent misuse as "real" footage.

What I Learned

Emotion is Subtle: The difference between "fake" and "real" isn't in the mouth movement—it's in the eyes and micro-jitters.

✓

Latency Matters: Even a perfect visual is broken if the response lags by 500ms. Interaction design must account for system performance.
